Biographical Sketch

Seema’s career spans over three decades of work located at the interface of society, development, and ecosystems. Her career evolved from that of a field officer to field researcher, to a conceptual explorer, towards action research and advocacy.

Geographically, Seema’s engagements span across peri urban zones, production landscapes and forest peripheries. By now, Seema has spent 12 years at the University, integrating concepts and realities in teaching, inclusive action research and in advocacy.

A bachelor’s degree (1982−86) from Kerala Agricultural University provided her with a multi-disciplinary base that kindled a strong aptitude towards Economics, especially Indian economy. Leaving a position with the Government of Kerala, she then pursued a master's degree in Agricultural Economics authoring her dissertation on the economic impact of industrial pollution on farmers. This published work trained Seema’s interest towards environmental Economics, then a newly emerging field.

In 1990, while working at the Institute of Economic Growth as a Research Assistant, Seema secured a Ford Foundation scholarship to join the Environmental Economics programme at University College London. That was the beginning of a long journey with the concept and practice of sustainability.

Her journey as an environmental economist became visible in 1991, through a global scale economic valuation of tropical forests, co-authored with Prof. David Pearce. This pioneering work was followed by a decade long involvement in water and forest related issues in various parts of India. Applying Environmental Economics also to study adivasi communities of southern India, culminated in her doctoral thesis on the economics of tribal land use. This was the beginning of her engagements with the rights and livelihoods of indigenous communities.

Her empirical work and engagement with the discourse on sustainable development and association with networks like SANDEE (South Asian Network for Development and Environmental Economics), INSEE (Indian Society for Ecological Economics) and networks around rural and agrarian studies, and the Global Land Initiative of The United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) has been constantly infusing social-institutional, distributional, educational and political dimensions of sustainability in her work.

Nine years spent at ATREE (Ashoka Trust for Ecology and the Environment) from 2003 and 12 years at Azim Premji University till 2025 sharpened Seema’s interdisciplinary teaching skills. This lens was instrumental in consolidating her attention towards Ecological Economics. Long encounter with field realities in India and other parts of the Global South, together with her engagement in various academic networks, international organisations, Indian voluntary sector in development & sustainability as well as with small farmer community at the grassroots, catalysed the emergence of an action researcher cum real time educator in Seema.
